Kidney Stones: Causes & Prevention

Painful but usually preventable. Learn why stones form and the simple habits that keep them from coming back.

Home/Education/Kidney Stones

A kidney stone is a hard deposit that forms when minerals in the urine become too concentrated and crystallise. Small stones may pass unnoticed, but larger ones can cause intense pain. Once you have had a stone, your chance of another is high — which is why prevention matters.

Types of kidney stones

TypeOften linked to
Calcium oxalate (most common)Low fluid intake, high oxalate/salt diet
Uric acidHigh animal-protein diet, gout, acidic urine
StruviteCertain urinary infections
Cystine (rare)An inherited condition

Symptoms of an attack

  • Severe, cramping pain in the side or back that comes in waves
  • Pain spreading to the lower abdomen and groin
  • Blood in the urine (pink, red or brown)
  • Nausea, vomiting, or a burning feeling when urinating
  • Fever and chills (may signal infection — seek urgent care)

Seven ways to prevent stones

  1. Drink more water. Aim for enough fluid to pass about 2–2.5 litres of pale urine a day — the single most effective step.
  2. Cut back on salt. High sodium pushes more calcium into the urine.
  3. Don't over-restrict calcium. Normal dietary calcium actually helps bind oxalate in the gut. Get calcium from food rather than supplements unless advised.
  4. Limit high-oxalate foods if you form calcium-oxalate stones (spinach, nuts, beetroot).
  5. Moderate animal protein to lower uric-acid stone risk.
  6. Add citrus. Lemon and lime juice raise urinary citrate, a natural stone inhibitor.
  7. Maintain a healthy weight and stay active.

Catch the stone

If you pass a stone, try to collect it. Laboratory analysis of its composition lets your doctor tailor prevention precisely to your stone type.

How are stones treated?

Many small stones pass on their own with fluids and pain relief. Larger or stuck stones may need procedures such as shock-wave lithotripsy (ESWL), ureteroscopy, or keyhole surgery. Your urologist or nephrologist will advise based on the stone's size and location.
