Frequently Asked Questions

What does a nephrologist do?
A nephrologist is a doctor who specialises in kidney health. We diagnose and treat conditions such as chronic kidney disease, high blood pressure, electrolyte disorders and kidney failure, and manage dialysis and kidney transplantation.
When should I see a nephrologist?
Consider a review if you have diabetes or high blood pressure, protein or blood in your urine, a low or falling eGFR, recurrent kidney stones, unexplained swelling, or a family history of kidney disease.

Can chronic kidney disease be reversed?
Most CKD cannot be cured, but early diagnosis and treatment can slow or halt its progression and prevent complications. Controlling blood pressure, blood sugar and diet is key. See our CKD guide.
What are the early warning signs of kidney disease?
Early kidney disease often has no symptoms. As it advances you may notice swelling in the feet or ankles, foamy urine, frequent night-time urination, fatigue, or poor appetite. Simple blood and urine tests can detect it early — see understanding your kidney tests.
